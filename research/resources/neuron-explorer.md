## What it does
Steering-first neuron explorer for **Qwen/Qwen3.5-4B** (32 layers, 9216 SwiGLU neurons/layer, d=2560). For each MLP neuron in a layer it adds `dose * W_down[:, n]` at the MLP output (`mlp_out`) on a few prompts ({who, snake, orange} x {-0.7,+0.5,+0.8} of median residual norm, plus a free prompt at +3), keeps every greedy rollout, and ranks/labels neurons from what the model then writes. Runs via a steering fork of vLLM (RhizoNymph/vllm); HF hooks (`layer.mlp` forward / pre-forward) used for checks and `mlp_in` captures.

## Worth borrowing (and why)
1. **Random-direction controls as a null** (`sweep/rank.py`): rank neurons by word-cluster mass (summed Poisson z of top words vs. the average unit) and report `null_pct` = share of 256 random directions beaten. Cheap, honest baseline for our steering claims.
2. **Doses relative to the median residual norm** at that layer (not raw activation units): comparable across layers/neurons.
3. **Steer lens** (`sweep/steer_lens.py`): next-token delta-logprob and KL for +/-0.8 pushes on every neuron in one prefill pass; a fast screen before expensive rollouts. For addition-with-carry: screen neurons by KL/delta-logprob on the answer digit at carry vs. no-carry prompts, then do rollouts only on the top candidates.
4. **Pole-wise units + SwiGLU sign regimes** (`NOTES.md`): treat N+ and N- separately; split activations by sign(gate) x sign(up).
5. **Correct hook semantics** (`research_log.md`): additive at mlp_out == "add to neuron n" only without FFN layer scale; ablation / x k gain / clamp need a hook at the down_proj **input**. Directional clamp at mlp_out is *not* neuron ablation. Relevant to how we ablate.
6. **Degeneration metrics per dose**: distinct-4-gram ratio, length-capped share, mean chosen logprob, to separate a real effect from coherence collapse (cf. our H4 result).
7. **Causal search idea** (from Sauers tawny-owl, cited in NOTES): patch neuron values from a condition run into a base run and find the minimal set that flips behaviour; random-k ablation control.

## Caveats
- Word-frequency ranking on 3-4 prompts with greedy decoding: small samples, prompt-specific, no seeds/variance, no multiple-comparison handling beyond the random null.
- Labels are often generated by an LLM agent (`explorer/agent_prompt.md`, `agent_methodology.md`); not human-validated.
- Depends on a forked vLLM and a CUDA 13 pod; not Kaggle-ready. Main sweep is L22 plus random subsets of a few layers.
- `explorer/server.py` launches a `claude` CLI agent with shell access to your home folder; do not run it.
- Unlicensed; findings unreproduced by us.
